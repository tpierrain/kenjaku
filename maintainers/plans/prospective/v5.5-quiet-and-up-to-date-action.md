## S1 — An engine skill on a long-lived brain comes up to date again _(#115)_

**What the person using Kenjaku lives today.** On a brain that has crossed several releases, `/lint`,
`/consolidate` and their siblings **stop receiving updates for good**. The new version lands beside the
old one as `SKILL.md.new`, so nothing is lost and nothing is silent — but the report explains it with a
sentence that is not true: *"your customized lint skill was kept exactly as you wrote it"*, about a file
they have never opened. From then on every release arrives as homework.

**What they would live instead.** The skill simply comes up to date, the way it does on a brain
installed fresh, and the word *customized* is kept for files they really did edit.

### Why it happens, in one paragraph — and why HEAD's own tests cannot see it

A staged skill (`engine-skills/<name>/`, delivered into `.claude/skills/<name>/`) has **no recorded
provenance**. Its stand-in is the brain's **own** `engine-skills/` copy, read at update time
(`readStagedProvenance`, `scripts/lib/staged-skills.mjs`) — the question being *"is what is installed
still what we delivered?"*. That works while the two move together. An update that advances the
**staging** copy without the **installed** one makes them disagree **permanently**: from that update on,
`mergeVerdict` reads the installed file as an owner edit (`owner-edit-stands`), then hands out a `.new`
sidecar at every release, for ever. The first divergence happens on the two-pass update out of an **old**
engine — the old-parent/new-child path no suite here can observe (`CONVENTIONS` §10ter), which is why
HEAD's code is correct in isolation and the fleet is still wrong.

### The fix this plan recommends, and the one it rejects

**Recommended: prove the installed skill against what the engine REALLY published, not against the
brain's own copy of it.** The machinery already exists and is already trusted for exactly this job:
`scripts/lib/engine-fingerprints.json` records, per published version, the bytes of every merge-regime
file, and `healFromDisk` / `healOne` (`scripts/lib/engine-heal.mjs`) turn **membership in that table**
into a provable `recorded` sha. Today the table is scoped by `selectMergeFiles`, so the staged skills are
outside it. Widen the scope, and a contaminated brain's installed skill is recognised as *a version we
shipped* → `refresh`, not *an owner edit*.

- 🛑 **The property that must survive: the proof is MEMBERSHIP, never arithmetic.** A sha may be recorded
  only because the installed bytes are **recognised** in the table, never computed from the installed
  bytes themselves. The day that slips, a real owner edit reads as untouched and the next update
  clobbers it. `engine-heal.mjs`'s own header says it; widening its input may not weaken it.
- **Rejected: record real provenance for staged skills at install time.** It is the tidier design and it
  **heals nobody** — a brain already diverged stays diverged, and diverged brains are the entire
  population this issue is about.

## S2 — Three questions stop being asked _(#118)_

**The specification already exists and is not to be re-derived**:
[`maintainers/registers/gestures.md`](../../registers/gestures.md) § *The 🟢 list*, answered **yes to all
three** on 2026-09-13. The doctrine underneath is
[ADR 0043](../../decisions/0043-graduated-autonomy-and-plain-language.md): a gesture may go quieter only
because **every change the brain writes is committed to the owner's own history as it happens**, so
anything done unasked is one undo away.

- [x] **S2.1 — a note the engine cannot read has its header put straight** (`engine-skills/lint/SKILL.md`).
      _(2026-09-13)_ Tier **🟢**: it acts, and it is **named in the batched tidy-up summary**. It is the
      one finding that costs the owner *answers* rather than tidiness — until it is fixed, that note keeps
      answering searches with what it said weeks ago.
  - The 🟢 block now holds **two** gestures and says what separates them, because it is the only thing
    that does: the link repair is **silent** (a typo already gone is not news), this one is **named**.
    A 🟢 that nobody can see would be a different gesture from the one he accepted.
  - The v5.4 marker that said *"it stays 🔴 until the owner says otherwise"* is **gone**, and its
    absence is asserted. A skill carrying both the answer and the open question carries the question.
- [x] **S2.2 — renaming a universe rebuilds the search, announced** (`.claude/skills/switch/SKILL.md`).
      _(2026-09-13)_ Tier **🟡**: it says what it is about to do and starts; the owner can stop it. It
      costs minutes of compute and risks no note. **Renaming, not creating or deleting** — see
      § *What S2.2 turned out to be*: the specification's label was wrong about which lifecycle
      operation it meant, and the row it comes from (*"Confirm a full re-index, minutes of compute, no
      data at risk"*) has only ever had one match in that file.
  - The preflight stays exactly where it was — it is the **announcement**, and it always was; what
    changed is the sentence after it. *"Then ask them to confirm"* → the veto, and step 2 is now
    *"unless they stopped you"* rather than *"only after they confirm"*.
  - ⛔ **Deletion did not move, and a test says so out loud**: the typed name, in the owner's own
    terminal, on a script that refuses to run without one. A guard asserts the veto sentence appears
    **nowhere** in that section — announcing-then-erasing is the one thing this release must not
    become.
- [x] **S2.3 — the same when the note-watching was not running** (`engine-skills/rag/SKILL.md`).
      _(2026-09-13)_ Same shape, same tier.
  - The bullet also gained what it never said: **why it matters**. *"Not running"* is a status; *"the
    index has stopped following your notes, so a search can answer from what they said days ago"* is
    the cost, and it is the same cost as S2.1's unreadable note.
  - ⚠️ **Its neighbour is still an offer, and rightly so.** When the engine gates search behind a
    re-index of its own, the offer is the **engine's** and the skill relays it. The guard therefore
    judges **one bullet at a time**: a check spanning the whole step would have to forbid the word
    *offer* three lines below, where it is the correct behaviour.
- [x] **S2.4 — the two properties that make the above safe, proven rather than assumed.** _(2026-09-13)_
  - **Every write these three make lands in the brain's own history**, and it is true for three
    *different* reasons, which is why assuming it would have been wrong. `/lint` edits a note through
    the agent's own tools → the write-triggered commit. `/switch`'s rename moves every note of a
    universe **from a Bash script**, where that net never fires → the **end-of-turn sweep** catches it
    (the class removal of [#69](https://github.com/tpierrain/kenjaku/issues/69)). `/rag` re-indexes and
    **writes no note at all** → it needs no net, and the skill says so.
  - 🔍 **And the sweep had a hole exactly where S2.2 leans on it.** Its only test ran against a brain
    wired to a **remote**; push is **opt-in**, so a brain that pushes nowhere is not an edge case, it
    is most of them. A new test covers it — and it **discriminates**: putting the sweep behind the
    push turns it, and only it, red. Without that, the rename would have been un-undoable on precisely
    the brains that never wired a remote, and it could not have gone quiet.
  - **The announcement is not a question in disguise**, held against the **engine's own sentence**
    rather than a retyped copy: `VETO_CLOSING` is now a named export of
    `scripts/lib/batched-announcement.mjs`, and the three carriers (`/lint`'s batch, the rename, the
    watcher) are asserted to contain **that** string. If the core ever rewords it, all three go red
    together.
  - 🛑 **What the guard will not pretend to do**, written into its own header: it cannot judge whether
    a sentence is well written, and it does **not** forbid a question mark — these sections carry
    headings that are questions. The first draft *did* forbid the phrases *"shall I"* / *"may I"* and
    went red on the very sentence that bans them, because a skill's clearest way to forbid a phrase is
    to quote it. Those words are pinned where the sentence really exists: over `batchMessage`'s output.

> ⚠️ **The boundary that may never move** (ADR 0045, *Guardrails*): a **destructive** gesture keeps its
> **typed** confirmation. Nothing in S2 is destructive, and nothing in S2 makes deletion easier to reach.

## 📜 History, and the durable half

### What S2.2 turned out to be — measured, 2026-09-13

The specification says *"re-index after a universe is **created or deleted**"* — in the register's
🟢 list, and again in the public wording of [#118](https://github.com/tpierrain/kenjaku/issues/118).
**Read against the code, neither half of that label exists**, and the third operation nobody named is
the one that matches:

- **Creating** a universe re-indexes **nothing**, and cannot: `set-active-universe.mjs create`
  registers a name and re-points the pointer. A universe one second old holds no notes, so there is
  nothing to re-encode and no wait to authorise.
- **Deleting** one does re-index — inside `delete-universe.mjs`, **after** the owner has retyped the
  name, in their own terminal, on a script that refuses to run without one. That gesture is
  **destructive**, so ADR 0045's guardrail freezes it exactly where it is. Its row in the register is
  a separate `keep`.
- **Renaming** is the one left, and it is the one the register's row actually describes: *"Confirm a
  full re-index (minutes of compute, no data at risk)"*. `rename-universe.mjs` re-embeds every note
  of the universe, `switch/SKILL.md` runs a `--preflight` that says what it costs and then **asks
  them to confirm**, and the rename itself loses nothing (renaming back undoes it). Reversible,
  certain, minutes of compute → 🟡 by the gate, which is precisely what the row proposed and the
  owner accepted.

**So the accepted decision stands and only its label was wrong.** The gesture put to him — *stop
making me authorise a wait that risks no note* — is the one being implemented; it simply lives in the
