  `See [[meetings/2026-05-02-axion-migration]].` into `[[meetings/2026-05-02-aXiom-migration]]`,
  `corrections=1 protected=0`. A Markdown link target in the same text **is** protected. The reporter
  is right on every point.
- **It reaches the disk.** `vault-write-notice.mjs` is a **`PreToolUse`** hook on `Write|Edit` that
  returns `updatedInput` — it rewrites the tool's input, so the broken link is what gets written,
  then committed and pushed by the brain's own hooks.
- **The exposure is older than the report says.** The reporter found it on `v5.5.0`; the feature
  landed in **`v5.3.0`** (`71bbbcf`, issue #66). Three releases, not one.
- **The reporting half already exists.** `protectedDirective()` in `vault-write-notice.mjs` says what
  was deliberately left alone, so S1.2 makes the notice truthful for free.

## Why the fix is safe, and what it deliberately costs

- **`PROTECTED` can only ever subtract.** Every entry removes a span from rewriting; none can cause a
  rewrite. So this change cannot introduce a new correction anywhere.
- **The accepted cost, and it is the reporter's own framing:** `[[target|alias]]` has prose in its
  second half, and that prose stops being corrected too. **A lost spelling costs a word; a rewritten
  target costs a link.** Take the conservative side.
- **The alternative was rejected**: parsing `[[…]]` to protect only the target and still correct the
  alias. It buys one corrected word and adds a parser to a guard whose whole value is that it is
  dumb and total.

## Why this is a hotfix and not a queued fix

It is the failure class this product treats as the worst: **silent, and announced as a success.** The
notice says *"I corrected the spelling here"*, which is true of the prose and misleading about the
link — so the owner is told a write went well at the moment it broke something, and a second brain is
a trust artifact whose owner has stopped re-checking. Nothing in the vault or the status line would
surface it.
