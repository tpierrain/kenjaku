## Why #132 first — the owner's reasoning, recorded so it is not re-litigated

- **It reaches everyone.** The lockfiles ship with the engine, so every brain on v5.5.1 carries the
  same 12 findings and every owner reads the same alarming banner.
- **The banner tells them to report it upstream, and nobody upstream is watching.** That is the part
  that makes it a trust problem rather than a dependency problem.
- **It is largely mechanical**, so it lands fast and #131 can be attacked against an already-green
  branch.

## Constraints carried over, not to be re-derived

- **The suite runs on implementation, never on record-keeping.** Plans and roadmaps are path-ignored;
  a documentation change is a conversation with the owner, not a silent addition to that filter.
- **Every push is followed by reading its result.** A red stops the next commit.
- **A guard is written test-first and judges no prose.** S1.3 in particular: a `paths-ignore`-shaped
  net that disables itself in silence is the exact failure this repo has already paid for once.
- **Release prose is written for non-developers first, and is never alarmist** — which is the whole
  subject of #132's banner.
