## What this release is about

> ### When your brain fixes a spelling for you, it no longer breaks the links in the note it is writing.

You can tell your brain a handful of things that are always true in a sphere of your working life: how a client's name is really spelled, and the versions of it you keep seeing in transcripts. From then on, when it writes a note for you, it puts the spelling right and says so. It deliberately leaves some passages alone — quoted words, code, the address inside a link — because correcting those would falsify them rather than tidy them.

The vault's own way of linking one note to another, `[[like this]]`, was missing from that list. So if one of your note **filenames** happened to carry a spelling you had declared wrong, every link pointing at it was quietly rewritten, and the link then led nowhere. Worse, the message you saw said the spelling had been corrected: the breakage was announced as a success, in a note you had no reason to re-check.

It was reported from a real vault the day its owner declared their first spellings, and six links broke at once. Anyone who has declared spellings since v5.3.0 could be affected; anyone who has not declared any never was.

## What you get

**Every time your brain writes or edits a note**

- 🔗 **Links are left alone, always.** A `[[link to another note]]` is an address, like the address inside any other link, and your brain now treats it as one. The prose around it is still corrected, exactly as before.
- 👀 **It tells you what it left as written.** Instead of claiming a correction it did not make, it says it left the passage alone, so you can decide whether it matters.

**If you have already declared spellings, once after updating**

- 🩹 **Ask your brain to tidy your notes (`/lint`) and it lists the links that lead nowhere.** This release stops new links from breaking; it cannot repair the ones already on disk, and that check is how you find them.

## What you have to do

Ask your brain to update its engine (`/update-engine`), then restart Claude once. Your notes, your keys and your constitution are untouched, as always.

## Under the hood

**One entry in a list that could only ever subtract.** The guard keeps a set of protected spans — frontmatter, fenced and inline code, a Markdown link's target, an autolink, blockquotes, quoted material in three typographies — and a declared spelling is never applied inside one. `[[…]]` now sits beside them. Every entry in that list removes text from rewriting and none can cause a rewrite, so the change cannot introduce a correction anywhere; it can only spare one.

**The span is bounded to a single line**, so a half-typed `[[` cannot exempt every paragraph below it — a test says so, and it is the one that kills the mutant that would have removed that bound.

**`[[target|alias]]` is spared whole, alias included, and that cost is deliberate.** The second half of such a link is prose, and it stops being corrected. It is the reporter's own framing: a lost spelling costs a word, a rewritten target costs a link. Parsing the pipe to correct only the alias was rejected — it buys one word and adds a parser to a guard whose entire value is being dumb and total.

**The truthful report needed no new surface.** The write notice already had a sentence for what it deliberately left alone; the fix simply makes that sentence the one an owner sees in this case.

**The exposure is three releases old, not one.** It was reported on v5.5.0; the declared-spellings feature landed in v5.3.0, and every release since carried it.

**Proof.** Four tests on the guard — the reported repro, the alias, the prose on both sides of a link, an unclosed `[[` — plus one end to end on the hook that rewrites the write, each of them red first for the right reason. Mutation pass on the changed file: **94.70 %**, every mutant on the new span killed. The one killable survivor it surfaced was pre-existing and is now closed with no production change: nothing asserted that `never : Axion`, with the space before the colon that French typing puts there, yields a clean spelling.
