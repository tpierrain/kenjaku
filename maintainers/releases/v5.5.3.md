## What this release is about

> ### When you change universe, your brain only tells you it still remembers what it actually read.

A universe is one sphere of your working life, such as a client or an employer. When you switch from one to another, your brain searches in the new one from that moment on. What it cannot do is forget what it already read earlier in the same conversation, so it says so: that one sentence is what keeps an answer from looking scoped to the new universe when it is not.

The sentence did not know what the conversation had read. It only knew which universe was active before, often left there by an earlier conversation. So a brand-new conversation whose very first message was `/switch` could be told *"I still have everything I read in 'acme'"* about a universe it had never touched. Nothing from that universe was loaded and nothing cost an extra token: the sentence itself was the whole mistake. It was also silent in one case where it should have spoken, when the universe read in was left two switches earlier.

This release makes the sentence true. Your brain now keeps a small private record of which universes each conversation searched in, and the switch reads it.

## What you get

**Every time you switch universe (`/switch`)**

- 🤐 **A conversation that has read nothing is told nothing.** Start a fresh conversation, switch, and the confirmation no longer claims a memory it does not have.
- 🧠 **A conversation that has read something is told exactly what.** The sentence names every universe you searched in that is now out of scope, even one you left two switches ago, and never names the one you are arriving in.
- 🌐 **A search across all your universes is disclosed as such.** If you asked to *"search all universes"* earlier, the switch says so rather than guessing names.
- 🤔 **When your brain cannot know, it says it conditionally.** Before the restart that follows an update, it says *"if I read anything in 'acme' earlier in this conversation, it is still in my memory"*, which is true either way.

## What you have to do

Ask your brain to update its engine (`/update-engine`), then restart Claude once. Your notes, your keys and your constitution are untouched, as always.

## Under the hood

**A recorder, not a guess.** A new hook, `scripts/conversation-reads.mjs`, answers two moments. When a conversation begins with an empty window (a new conversation, or `/clear`), it opens an empty entry under that conversation's session id. After every `search_vault` and `get_document`, it adds the universe that read ran in (`*` for an all-universes search). It never speaks, never blocks, and always exits 0; its file lives in the brain's gitignored `.cache/`, keeps the fifty most recent conversations, and never travels with the brain. The universe is read through the validated pointer reader, so a pointer aimed at a deleted universe is recorded as the scope the search really used.

**The switch knows which conversation it is in.** Claude Code exports the same session id to the shell a skill runs (`CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`) that it hands every hook, and it changes on `/clear`. The switch reads the entry for that id only; another conversation's reads are never counted.

**The record only vouches for a window it saw begin.** A conversation with an entry is known: the reminder names the named universes it read, minus the destination and the cross-cutting scope, or says nothing. A conversation without one is not: it started before the hook was active (the gap between an update and the restart), or it was resumed after its entry was pruned. A read there is not recorded either, since a partial list would name some universes and hide others. Those conversations, and a shell with no session id, get the conditional sentence, under the same rule as before: silent when leaving the cross-cutting scope, spoken when leaving a named universe.

**It reaches existing brains through the usual door.** The hook is added to `settings.json`, on both events, by the update's additive hook reconcile. Rehearsed on copies of two real brains, one installed at v3.4.0 and one at v5.5.1 with 720 notes: both report the hook as newly wired, and the owner's territory stays byte-identical.

**Left out on purpose.** A vault note opened with the plain `Read` tool is not recorded, since the brain reads through its own search; and a `get_document` is filed under the universe active at the time rather than the note's own frontmatter.

**Proof.** The reported case is a test that was red first: a fresh conversation, `/switch`, no search, no residue sentence, run end to end through the real command with a real session id. Beside it, the reads being named, the two-switches-ago case, the all-universes case, and both fallbacks. Mutation pass on what the branch wrote: **95.33 %** on the second pass (both new files, and every changed hunk at 100 %); the five mutants left alive are equivalent, and the two redundant guards the first pass pointed at were deleted rather than tested.

Thanks to @chrisdeniaud for reporting it, with the exact sentence and the question that made the fix obvious: *does this cost tokens?* It did not, and now it does not say anything untrue either.
